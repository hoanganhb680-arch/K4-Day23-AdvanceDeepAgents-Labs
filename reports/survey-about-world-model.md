# World Models: From Latent-Dynamics Agents to Foundation Simulators

## TL;DR
- World models are internal predictive models of an environment that let agents plan, imagine, and learn inside a learned simulation; the modern line runs from Ha & Schmidhuber's "World Models" through PlaNet/Dreamer/MuZero to today's video-generation foundation models [1][2][3][4].
- Latent-dynamics model-based RL delivers dramatic sample-efficiency gains: PlaNet solves continuous-control tasks from pixels using substantially fewer episodes than model-free baselines, and DreamerV2 was the first agent to reach human-level Atari performance by learning purely inside a world model [2][5].
- Since 2023, world modeling has shifted to large-scale generative systems: Genie (11B) learns action-controllable worlds from unlabeled internet video, GameNGen simulates DOOM at 20 FPS, and Genie 3 generates real-time interactive worlds at 24 FPS [4][6][7].
- World models are now deployed as simulators and planners in robotics and autonomous driving, e.g., UniSim trains zero-shot sim-to-real policies, and Epona's learned world model doubles as a real-time motion planner on NAVSIM [8][9].
- Evaluation evidence is sobering: video models generalize case-based rather than rule-based, fail out-of-distribution physics, and even frontier VLMs perform near-random on basic motion-trajectory discrimination [10][11].

## Background
A world model is an internal simulator that learns the structure and dynamics of an environment, enabling an agent to predict, plan, and reason within learned representations [12]. In model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL), it is often formalized narrowly as a learned transition function plus reward predictor; in the broader AI literature it is increasingly viewed as a general-purpose simulator supporting counterfactual reasoning, causal inference, and hierarchical planning [12][13]. The core promise is imagination: once a model can simulate likely futures under candidate actions, an agent can evaluate plans or train partly or entirely inside the learned environment rather than the real one [12]. MBRL surveys trace this idea back to Samuel's checkers programme, Dyna, and AlphaGo Zero, and argue the transition model acts as a "multiplier" on the information extracted from each environment sample — a key reason world models matter for sample efficiency [13].

The term "world model" was popularized in deep learning by Ha & Schmidhuber (2018), who trained a VAE + MDN-RNN + controller pipeline and showed a compact policy could be trained entirely inside the model's hallucinated "dream" and then transferred back to the real environment [1][14]. The topic matters now because large-scale video generation (Sora, Genie, Cosmos) has reframed world models as candidate "general-purpose simulators of the physical world," while robotics and autonomous driving companies adopt them as trainable, controllable environments [15][16][17].

## Foundations: Latent Dynamics, Imagination, and Predictive Abstraction
The first wave of deep world models learned compact latent dynamics from pixels. PlaNet introduced the Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) — a latent dynamics model with both deterministic and stochastic transition components — and "latent overshooting," a multi-step variational objective that trains all multi-step predictions in latent space [2]. Planning happened by CEM search over action sequences in latent space, avoiding image generation, and the agent solved six continuous-control tasks from pixels within about 100 episodes, using substantially fewer episodes than model-free algorithms [2]. These ideas built on earlier latent state-space models such as DVBF, which learned Markovian state-space models from raw image data via stochastic variational Bayes [18].

The Dreamer family replaced online planning with "latent imagination": an actor-critic learns by propagating analytic value gradients back through trajectories imagined in the compact latent space of a fixed world model [19]. DreamerV1 reached an average score of 823 on 20 DeepMind Control Suite tasks after 5x10^6 steps, exceeding model-free D4PG [19]. DreamerV2 introduced discrete(categorical) latent representations and KL balancing, becoming the first agent to achieve human-level performance on Atari by learning behaviors purely inside a separately trained world model; during 200M environment steps it imagined about 468B compact states, roughly 10,000x more than the 50M real inputs [5]. DreamerV3 generalized the recipe to over 150 tasks across 8 domains with a single fixed configuration, and was the first algorithm to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch without human data or curricula [20].

A parallel line, MuZero, showed that a learned model need not predict observations at all: it combines Monte-Carlo tree search with a model predicting only the policy, value, and reward, achieving superhuman performance on all 57 Atari games and matching AlphaZero on Go, chess, and shogi without being given the rules [3]. SimPLe, by contrast, demonstrated pixel-space video-prediction world models for Atari in the low-data 100k-interaction regime, outperforming model-free algorithms in most games [21]. These approaches differ along a key axis: how much of the environment the model must reconstruct (pixels vs latent vs planning-relevant quantities only), which determines both sample efficiency and vulnerability to model bias — planning with a biased model can actively harm the policy [13].

A distinct, non-generative school is the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture(JEPA) proposed by LeCun: rather than predicting pixels, a JEPA predicts the representation of a future input from the representation of a context input, dropping irrelevant detail and enabling longer-horizon prediction [22]. I-JEPA showed this works for images, converging in roughly 5x fewer iterations than pixel-reconstruction methods like MAE [23]; V-JEPA extended feature prediction to video, with a ViT-H/16 trained only on videos reaching 81.9% on Kinetics-400 [24]. JEPA-style models are thus world models that predict in representation space rather than generating observations — a design choice at the center of an ongoing debate about whether generative reconstruction or latent prediction is the right objective [25].

## Large-Scale Generative World Models: Video and Games
Since 2023 the field has scaled world models to billions of parameters trained on internet-scale video. Genie, at 11B parameters, was the first generative interactive environment trained unsupervised from unlabelled internet videos: a spatiotemporal video tokenizer plus autoregressive dynamics model plus a simple latent action model lets users act frame-by-frame in generated worlds, and the learned latent action space enables training agents to imitate behaviors from unseen videos [4]. Genie 2 extended this to action-controllable, playable 3D environments from a single prompt image, using an autoregressive latent diffusion model that keeps worlds consistent for up to a minute and remembers off-screen parts of the world; it was tested with DeepMind's SIMA agent following natural-language instructions [26].

OpenAI's Sora technical report framed scaling video generation as "a promising path towards building general purpose simulators of the physical world," reporting emergent 3D consistency, object permanence, and simple world interactions from a diffusion transformer trained on spacetime patches — while acknowledging it does not accurately model physics of many basic interactions [15]. That framing ignited the "is Sora a world simulator?" debate [27][28]. Meanwhile, diffusion models became real-time game engines: GameNGen simulated DOOM at 20 FPS on a single TPU with next-frame PSNR of 29.4, such that human raters were only slightly better than chance at distinguishing simulation from game clips [6]; DIAMOND trained an RL agent entirely within a diffusion world model, reaching a mean human-normalized score of 1.46 on Atari 100k [29]. Open-source interactive Minecraft world models followed: Oasis runs at 20 FPS with a 500M-parameter open-weight model [30], MineWorld generates 4-7 FPS via parallel decoding [31], and Matrix-Game scales to over 17B parameters with fine-grained action annotations [32].

Video-generation companies also adopted the world-model framing: Runway positioned Gen-3 Alpha as"a step towards building General World Models," with temporally dense captions for fine-grained control [33]. On the video-prediction side, V-JEPA 2 pre-trained on over 1 million hours of internet video (300M-1B parameters) and, after action-conditioned post-training on under 62 hours of unlabeled robot video, was deployed zero-shot for pick-and-place via planning with image goals [34]. NVIDIA's Cosmos platform packages open-weight World Foundation Models (diffusion and autoregressive transformers) that can be fine-tuned into customized world models for robotics and autonomous driving [16]. UniSim, a 5.6B-parameter video diffusion model, learns a "universal simulator" of real-world interaction under a unified action-in-video-out interface and was used to train policies that transfer zero-shot to real robots [8]. The Genie line continued into robotics: Genie Envisioner unifies policy learning, evaluation, and simulation (GE-Base/GE-Act/GE-Sim)[35]; GE-Sim 2.0 tops the public WorldArena leaderboard at only 2B parameters [36]; and GE-Act 2.0 shows scaling co-training data from 300 to  30,000 hours raises zero-shot success from 17.1% to 44.1% [37].

## World Models for Robotics and Embodied AI
In robotics, world models serve as learned simulators, policy learners, and evaluation environments [38]. Video generative pre-training has proven effective: GR-1, a GPT-style model predicting actions and future images, improved CALVIN success from 88.9% to  94.9% and zero-shot unseen-scene generalization from  53.3% to  85.4% after large-scale video pretraining [39]. RT-2 established the vision-language-action(VLA) paradigm by co-fine-tuning VLMs on robot data and internet-scale vision-language tasks, expressing actions as text tokens and yielding emergent generalization to novel objects and rudimentary reasoning [40]. Video PreTraining(VPT) showed that unlabeled online video plus a small amount of labeled data can produce behavioral priors capable of hard-exploration tasks such as crafting diamond tools in Minecraft [41].

A key architectural question is whether policies should explicitly model how the environment changes under the agent's own intervention. World Action Models(WAMs) are defined as embodied predictive-action models that make a forecast of the future available to action; recent WAMs repurpose large video generation models, while a parallel line uses language/vision-language backbones without a video-generation core, and the field is moving toward "dreaming less" — generating less of the future while preserving what control requires [42]. UniSim demonstrated the simulator route: training high-level vision-language planners and low-level RL policies purely in simulation that then deploy zero-shot on real robots [8]. V-JEPA 2-AC similarly showed an action-conditioned latent world model can plan zero-shot for pick-and-place via planning with image goals, without environment-specific data or task-specific rewards [34].

## World Models for Autonomous Driving
Autonomous driving has become a major application domain, with dedicated surveys categorizing Driving World Models(DWMs) by predicted scene modality: video, point cloud, occupancy, latent feature, and traffic map [43]. Wayve's GAIA-1 casts world modeling as unsupervised sequence modeling over video, text, and action tokens, offering fine-grained control over ego-vehicle behavior and scene features [17]; GAIA-2 is a latent diffusion world model unifying multi-agent interactions, fine-grained control, and multi-camera consistency across geographically diverse driving environments [44]. DriveDreamer was described as the first world model established entirely from real-world driving scenarios, using a diffusion model anda two-stage training pipeline faithful to traffic structural constraints [45]; DriveDreamer-2 adds an LLM interface that converts user queries into agent trajectories and generates customized driving videos, surpassing prior methods with FID 11.2 and FVD 55.7 [46].

Beyond generation, world models are increasingly coupled with planning. UniAD established the planning-oriented end-to-end paradigm, integrating perception, prediction, and planning in one network via unified query interfaces [47]. Epona, an autoregressive diffusion world model with decoupled spatiotemporal factorization, achieves state-of-the-art video generation(7.4% FVD improvement)and its learned world model also serves as a real-time motion planner, outperforming strong end-to-end planners on NAVSIM [9]. DriveDreamer-Policy unifies depth generation, future video generation, and motion planning in a single modular architecture, reaching 89.2 PDMS on Navsim v1 [48]. These systems illustrate a broader trend: world models for driving are shifting from pure video synthesis toward closed-loop, geometry-grounded planning [43][48].

## Evaluation, Benchmarks, and the "Are They World Models?" Debate
Evaluation of world models has moved beyond video-quality metrics. WorldModelBench grades video generation models on instruction following plus physics adherence across 7 application-driven domains, using 67K crowd-sourced human labels to fine-tune a 2B-parameter VLM judger [49]. WBench evaluates interactive video world models across 5 dimensions(video quality, setting adherence, interaction adherence, consistency, physics compliance)with 22 automatic sub-metrics over 1,058 interaction turns, finding that no single model dominates all dimensions [50]. WorldBench tests disentangled, concept-specific physics: rollouts often look right(ball follows a parabola)while physical constants are wrong, recovered physical parameters are biased and high-variance, and foreground mIoU correlates with human judgments of motion quality[51]. WorldTest/AutumnBench proposes a two-phase, behavior-based protocol separating reward-free interaction from scored test phases, finding that 517 human participants outperform three frontier reasoning models [52]. WM-ABench atomically evaluates VLMs as world models across 23 dimensions, finding almost all models perform at near-random accuracy when distinguishing motion trajectories [11].

The debate over whether video generators or LLMs are genuine world models is central. A physical-law study trained DiT video models on a 2D mechanics testbed and found perfect in-distribution generalization but failure out-of-distribution: models exhibit"case-based" generalization, prioritizing factors as color > size > velocity > shape, leading the authors to conclude scaling alone is insufficient to uncover fundamental physical laws [10]. Millière's analysis distinguishes a strong sense of world simulator(running simulations over actions/observations/rewards)from a weak sense(learning structure-preserving representations), arguing Sora is unlike model-based-RL world models and that behavioral evidence is insufficient to assess the claim [28]. LLM studies add further skepticism: grid-world maze experiments probe whether LLMs build spatial world models [53], and MentalMap, a multilingual diagnostic, finds a universal"L3 reasoning cliff" where no model retains even half of its baseline accuracy on viewpoint reasoning [54]. A "Critique of World Model" essay argues the primary goal of a world model is simulating actionable possibilities for reasoning and acting, not generating videos, and criticizes JEPA-style latent prediction for"indefinability" while proposing a Generative Latent Prediction architecture [25]. Surveys of the field frame these tensions as a choice between"understanding the world" and"predicting the future" [55][27].

## Trends and open problems
The last two years have seen three converging trends: (1) world models scaled into foundation-model territory trained on internet-scale video(Genie 2, V-JEPA 2, Cosmos, Matrix-Game)[26][34][16][32];(2) a shift from pure generation toward closed-loop, action-conditioned systems that couple simulation with planning and policy learning(GE-Sim 2.0, Epona, DriveDreamer-Policy)[36][9][48];and(3) a wave of benchmarks attempting to measure physical understanding, interaction fidelity, and decision-making utility rather than visual quality alone [49][50][51][52].

Open problems remain substantial. Compounding error in autoregressive rollouts limits long-horizon consistency: even Genie 3, the first real-time interactive world model generating photorealistic worlds at  24 FPS, retains consistency only for a few minutes and cannot simulate real-world locations with perfect accuracy [7]. Physical understanding is shallow: models fail out-of-distribution physics, recover biased physical parameters, and lack causal understanding [51][10]. Whether scaling alone can discover physical laws is disputed — controlled studies say no for out-of-distribution generalization [10], while frontier labs continue scaling world models as a path to general-purpose simulators [15][16]. The right learning objective is contested: generative reconstruction vs JEPA-style latent prediction vs planning-relevant quantities [3][25]. Finally, whether LLMs/VLMs can serve as general-purpose world models remains largely unsupported by current evidence, motivating multimodal and scratchpad-augmented reasoning as future directions [53][54][11].
